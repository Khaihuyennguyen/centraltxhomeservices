ADU & Garage Conversion Guide for Temple, Waco & Central Texas (2026)

Published for Homeowners in Temple, Waco, Belton, Hewitt, and Round Rock, TX · Updated August 2026

The demand for backyard rental units and in-law suites in Central Texas has doubled in the last 3 years. If you own a home in Temple, Waco, Belton, or the surrounding Bell/McLennan county area, converting your garage or building a small ADU (Accessory Dwelling Unit) is now one of the highest-ROI home projects available — often paying itself back in 6-8 years through rental income.

This guide covers what it actually costs, how long it takes, what permits you need, and how to know if your specific lot qualifies. All numbers are current for 2026.

1. What Is an ADU and Why Central Texas Cities Are Encouraging Them

An ADU is a secondary dwelling on a single-family property — either attached to the main home, converted from an existing garage, or built as a separate small structure in the yard. In the Temple/Waco corridor, ADUs are increasingly permitted because they address rental housing shortages without changing neighborhood character.

Common ADU forms in Central Texas:

  • Garage conversion (most popular): Convert an existing attached or detached garage into a rentable unit. Reuses existing footprint and foundation.
  • Attached ADU addition: Add a small unit as an extension of the main house with a separate entrance.
  • Detached backyard unit: Small standalone structure (400-800 sq ft) in the backyard.

3. Rental ROI in the Temple/Waco Corridor

Central Texas rental demand has stayed strong. Here's what ADUs in the corridor actually rent for in 2026:

  • Long-term unfurnished (400-600 sq ft): $900-$1,500/month
  • Long-term furnished: $1,300-$1,800/month
  • Short-term rental (Airbnb near Baylor or Temple hospitals): $1,800-$2,500/month gross

Sample payback math: $90,000 basic garage conversion renting for $1,200/month = $14,400/year gross. After taxes, insurance, and maintenance (~30%), you net roughly $10,000/year. Payback: ~9 years, but appreciation on the improved property typically adds 15-25% to your home value, closing that gap in year 6-7.

4. Permit Process in Bell & McLennan Counties

Both counties allow ADUs on most single-family lots, but you must verify your specific zoning. General timeline:

  1. Week 1-2: Site survey, zoning verification, feasibility check
  2. Week 2-4: Architectural drawings + engineering stamps
  3. Week 4-8: Permit application submitted, city plan review
  4. Week 8-12: Permit approval, construction begins

Simple garage conversions on properties with existing utilities and no setback issues move fastest. Lots requiring variance requests or new sewer/water hookups can add 60+ days.

5. Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the feasibility check. Some lots have setback rules, easements, or HOA restrictions that make an ADU illegal. Confirm before you spend $5k+ on drawings.
  • Under-budgeting for electrical. Adding a full kitchen means upgrading the main panel — usually a $3,000-$8,000 add nobody warns you about.
  • Using unlicensed contractors. Bell and McLennan counties require licensed electrical/plumbing/HVAC. Unpermitted work will fail inspection and destroy resale value.
  • Forgetting insurance and property tax reassessment. Once you add a habitable unit, your property tax basis increases and your homeowner's insurance needs a rider.
